Introduction

What Are Crowns & Bridges?

Crowns and bridges are fixed prosthetic devices cemented onto existing teeth or implants. Unlike dentures, which you can take out and clean daily, crowns and bridges can only be removed by a dentist.

Dental Crowns

A crown (or \"cap\") completely covers a damaged, weak, or severely decayed tooth to restore its original shape, size, strength, and appearance. It is typically used after a root canal or when a large cavity threatens the health of the tooth.

Dental Bridges

A bridge is used to replace one or more missing teeth. It consists of an artificial tooth (pontic) anchored between two crowns placed on the healthy teeth (abutments) on either side of the gap.

Types of Crowns

High-quality materials. Natural results. Long-lasting smiles.

METAL CROWN

Extremely durable and long-lasting. Ideal for back teeth that handle heavy chewing forces.

  • Highly durable
  • Resistant to wear and fracture
  • Ideal for back molars
  • Cost-effective
IDEAL FOR: Back teeth and molars where strength is the top priority.
PFM CROWN

Porcelain fused to metal for strength and natural appearance. A perfect balance of durability and aesthetics.

  • Strong and durable
  • Natural tooth-colored appearance
  • Good for front and back teeth
  • More affordable than all-ceramic
IDEAL FOR: Front and back teeth where strength and aesthetics are needed.
ZIRCONIA CROWN

Made from high-strength zirconia ceramic. Extremely durable with excellent natural appearance.

  • Exceptional strength
  • Highly aesthetic
  • Biocompatible & metal-free
  • Ideal for all teeth
IDEAL FOR: Both front and back teeth. Best all-round performance.
E-MAX CROWN

Premium all-ceramic crown known for its lifelike translucency and beautiful aesthetics.

  • Superior aesthetics
  • Natural translucency
  • Metal-free & biocompatible
  • Perfect for front teeth
IDEAL FOR: Front teeth and visible areas for the best aesthetics.

When Are They Needed?

Crowns and bridges solve a wide variety of dental issues, preventing further decay and restoring functionality.

  • To protect a weak tooth from fracturing after a root canal.
  • To cover a dental implant.
  • To replace a large filling when there isn't enough tooth remaining.
  • To fill the gap of a missing tooth (Bridge) to stop teeth from shifting.

The Procedure

Our Treatment Process

Preparation

The tooth is numbed, and a thin layer of enamel is shaved off to make room for the crown to fit over it perfectly.

Digital Impressions

We take a precise 3D scan of your mouth and send the data to our dental lab to custom-mill your crown or bridge.

Temporary Placement

While your permanent crown is being made (usually takes a few days), we place a temporary acrylic crown to protect your tooth.

Final Cementation

On your second visit, we remove the temporary, check the fit and color of the permanent crown, and cement it permanently in place.

Maintenance

Aftercare Tips

Normal Hygiene

Brush and floss your crowned teeth exactly like natural teeth to prevent decay at the gum line.

Flossing Bridges

Use special floss threaders or a water flosser to clean underneath the artificial tooth of a bridge.

Avoid Hard Foods

Avoid chewing ice, hard candies, or using your teeth to open packages, which can chip porcelain.
