What is a Root Canal?

A root canal (endodontic therapy) is a treatment used to repair and save a tooth that is badly decayed or infected. During the procedure, the nerve and pulp inside the tooth are removed, and the inside of the tooth is thoroughly cleaned and sealed.

Why is it Needed?

Without treatment, the tissue surrounding the tooth will become heavily infected, and abscesses may form, leading to intense pain, facial swelling, and eventually, the loss of the tooth. A root canal entirely removes the source of the infection while allowing you to keep the structure of your natural tooth.

Signs You Need an RCT

If the nerve of your tooth is infected, you will likely experience one or more of these symptoms:

Severe Tooth Pain

Constant, throbbing ache, especially when biting or at night.

Temperature Sensitivity

Lingering pain after eating hot or cold foods.

Swollen Gums

Tender, swollen, or darkened gums near the painful tooth.

Abscess (Pimple)

A recurring pimple on the gums that may ooze pus.

Common Causes

The pulp (nerve) of the tooth can become inflamed or infected due to several reasons:

Deep Tooth Decay

Untreated cavities that reach the inner pulp chamber.

Cracked/Broken Tooth

Physical cracks allow bacteria to enter the nerve.

Dental Trauma

A blow to the mouth can damage the nerve even without visible cracks.

Repeated Procedures

Multiple deep fillings on the same tooth over time.

Benefits of Root Canal Treatment

Saves the Natural Tooth

Eliminates Pain

The Procedure

Our patient care process for a pain-free experience.

1. X-Ray & Diagnosis

Mapping the shape of the root canals.

2. Local Anesthesia

Numbing the tooth completely for a painless procedure.

3. Cleaning & Disinfection

Removing the infected nerve tissue.

4. Root Canal Filling

Sealing the roots with bio-compatible gutta-percha.

5. Tooth Restoration

Placing a core build-up filling.

6. Crown Placement

Protecting the tooth from future fracture with a durable crown.
