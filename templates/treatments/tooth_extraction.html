Introduction

What is Tooth Extraction?

A tooth extraction is the removal of a tooth from its socket in the bone. At Niko Dental & Aesthetics, our primary goal is always to save your natural teeth. However, when a tooth is too severely damaged to be repaired with a filling or root canal, extraction becomes necessary to protect your overall oral health.

Many patients feel anxious about having a tooth pulled. We understand this, which is why we use advanced numbing techniques and a gentle approach to ensure your extraction is as comfortable, swift, and stress-free as possible.

Understanding the Need

When is Extraction Required?

Reasons & Symptoms

  • Severe Decay: A cavity so deep that the tooth cannot be saved by a root canal.
  • Advanced Gum Disease: Periodontitis has destroyed the bone supporting the tooth, making it loose.
  • Orthodontic Crowding: Removing a tooth to create space for braces to align the remaining teeth.
  • Impacted Wisdom Teeth: Third molars that are stuck under the gums, causing pain and infection.
  • Severe Trauma: A tooth fractured below the gumline.

Types of Extractions

Simple Extraction

Performed on a tooth that can be seen in the mouth. We loosen the tooth with an instrument called an elevator and remove it with dental forceps. It is very quick and requires only local anesthesia.

Surgical Extraction

Required for a tooth that has broken off at the gum line or has not erupted yet (like a wisdom tooth). A small incision is made in the gum to access and carefully remove the tooth. Stitches may be placed afterward.

The Bright Side

Benefits of Timely Extraction

The Procedure

Step-by-Step Treatment Process

X-Ray Assessment

We take a digital X-ray to evaluate the tooth's roots, surrounding bone, and proximity to nerves.

Anesthesia

Strong local anesthesia is administered so you feel only pressure, not pain, during the procedure.

Extraction

The tooth is gently loosened with an elevator and carefully removed using forceps. If surgical, a small incision is made first.

Clot Formation

Gauze is placed over the socket. You will bite down to apply pressure, stopping the bleeding and helping a blood clot form.

Healing

Recovery & Aftercare

Proper aftercare is critical for a fast recovery and to prevent complications like 'dry socket'.

Bite on Gauze

Keep steady pressure on the gauze pad for 30-45 minutes immediately after the procedure to allow the blood clot to form.

Do NOT Spit or Use a Straw

For the first 24 hours, avoid aggressive rinsing, spitting, or drinking through a straw. The suction can dislodge the blood clot.

Apply Ice

Apply an ice pack to your cheek in 15-minute intervals to minimize swelling, especially after a surgical extraction.

Eat Soft Foods

Stick to soft foods (yogurt, soup, mashed potatoes) and avoid hot liquids on the first day.

FAQ

Frequently Asked Questions

We make sure you are completely numb before we begin. You will feel pressure during the procedure, but you should not feel any sharp pain. We also provide excellent pain management strategies for your recovery at home.

A dry socket occurs if the blood clot that forms in the socket is dislodged prematurely, exposing the underlying bone and nerves. This is very painful. You can prevent it by not smoking, using straws, or spitting forcefully for the first few days.

Except for wisdom teeth, it is highly recommended to replace an extracted tooth with an implant, bridge, or denture. Leaving a gap will cause your other teeth to shift, affecting your bite and appearance over time.

The initial healing period takes about 1 to 2 weeks, during which new gum tissue will grow over the socket. Complete bone healing takes several months.
